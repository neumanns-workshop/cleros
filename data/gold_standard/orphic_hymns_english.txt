  3. Zeus the king, and Gaia, and the holy heavenly flames
  4. of Hēlios, and the sacred radiance of Mēnē, and all the Stars;
  5. and you too, Poseidaon, earth-supporting, dark-maned,
  6. and holy Phersephonē, and Dēmētēr rich in fruit,
  7. and Artemis the arrow-showering, maiden, and gentle Phoibos,

  9. honors among the Blessed, Dionysos, lord of the dance;
 10. and Ares of stormy heart, and the holy might of Hēphaistos,
 11. and the foam-born goddess, possessor of high-renowned gifts;
 12. and you, king of those below, surpassing great daimon;
 13. and Hēbē, Eileithyia, and the goodly might of Hēraklēs;
 14. and the great boon of Dikaiosynē and Eusebeia
 15. I invoke, and the famed Nymphs, and great Pan,
 16. and Hērā, the flourishing bedfellow of aegis-bearing Zeus;
 17. and lovely Mnēmosynē, and I call upon the holy
 18. nine Mousai, and the Kharites, and the Hōrai, and Eniautos,
 19. and Lētō of the fair tresses, divine, and august Diōnē,

 21. and together the great Sōtēres, the imperishable children of Zeus;
 22. and the Idaian gods, and the messenger of the Ouranians,

 26. and Rheia, and Kronos, and Tēthys of the dark veil,
 27. and great Ōkeanos, together

 45. to this holy rite of burnt‑offering, and to the august libation.


HYMN 1: Ὕμνος εἰς Ἑκάτην.
-------------------------

  1. I invoke Hekate Einodia, of the three-way crossroads, lovely,
  2. heavenly, chthonian, and seaborne, saffron-robed,
  3. of the tombs, reveling Bacchically among the souls of the dead,
  4. Perseia, fond of solitude, exulting in deer,
  5. nocturnal, of the whelps, the unconquerable queen,
  6. Bull-driving, key-bearing queen of the whole cosmos,
  7. guide, nymph, nurse of youths, mountain-roving,
  8. being implored, Maiden, be present at holy rites for the suppliants,
  9. ever gracious to the herdsman, with a gladdened heart.

HYMN 2: Προθυραίας
------------------

  1. Hear me, O most-august goddess, much-named daimon,
  2. helper in birth-pangs, sweet in aspect to women in childbed;
  3. savior of women, sole child-loving one, gentle of mind,
  4. swift-delivering, a helper amid mortals’ pains, Prothyraia.
  5. key-bearing, propitious to meet, fond of nurture, gracious to all,
  6. you who hold the households of all, and you rejoice in banquets,
  7. girdle-loosener, unseen, yet in your works you appear to all,
  8. you share in the birth-pangs and you rejoice in easy deliveries,
  9. Eileithyia, loosening pains amid dreadful constraints.
 10. for women in childbed call upon you alone, the soul’s repose.
 11. for in you the sorrows of childbirth are made to be forgotten.
 12. Artemis Eileithyia, and
 13. revered Prothyraia,
 14. hear, blessed one; grant births, being present as a helper,
 15. and save, as you are by nature ever the savior before all.

HYMN 3: Νυκτός
--------------

  1. I shall sing of Nyx, begetress of gods and of men.
  2. Nyx, genesis of all things, whom we will also call Kypris.
  3. Hear, blessed goddess, dark-blue-gleaming, star-bright;
  4. rejoicing in quiet and in stillness rich in sleep;
  5. mirthful, delightful, lover of all-night vigils, mother of dreams;
  6. care-forgetting, possessing a good respite from toils;
  7. giver of sleep, beloved of all, chariot-driving, night-luminous;
  8. half-complete, chthonian, and again the same, heavenly;
  9. all-encircling, sporting in the chases of those who wander in mist;
 10. who send forth light beneath to the nether regions, and again you flee
 11. into Hades; for dread Anankê prevails over all.
 12. But now, O blessed Nyx, much-wealthy, longed-for by all,
 13. gracious in meeting, hearing the suppliant voice of my words,
 14. come benevolent, and send away the night-gleaming fears.

HYMN 6: Πρωτογόνου

HYMN 11: Πανός
--------------

  1. I call upon Pan, the mighty, the Pastoral One, the All of the cosmos,
  2. heaven and sea, and the all-sovereign earth,
  3. and immortal fire; for these are the members of Pan.
  4. Come, blessed one, leaper, all-ranging runner, co-throned with the Horai;
  5. goat-limbed, Bacchic reveler, lover of divine possession, star-dwelling;
  6. weaving the harmony of the cosmos with playful song,
  7. helper of visions, dread to mortal fears;
  8. rejoicing with goatherds by the springs and with cowherds,
  9. keen-sighted, hunter, friend of Echo, chorus-mate of the Nymphs;
 10. of every nature, begetter of all, many-named daimon;
 11. world-ruler, Increaser, Light-bringer, Fruitful Paian;
 12. cave-delighting, heavy in wrath, the true horned Zeus.
 13. For upon you the boundless expanse of earth is set firm,
 14. and the deep-flowing water of the untiring sea yields,
 15. and Okeanos, encircling, coils the earth round with his waters;
 16. and the airy portion of sustenance, the kindling for living beings,
 17. and, above the summit, the eye of the lightest fire.
 18. For these divine realms move, manifoldly parted, at your commands;
 19. and you alter the natures of all things by your providences,
 20. feeding the race of humans and the boundless cosmos as well.
 21. But come, blessed one, Bacchic reveler, lover of possession, upon the holy libations,
 22. and grant a good completion of life,
 23. sending forth the Pan-ic oistros to the bounds of the earth.

HYMN 19: Κεραυνίου Διός
-----------------------

 18. But, Blessed One, cast your heavy spirit into the waves of the sea,
 19. and upon the mountain peaks; we all know your might.
 20. Yet, in return for the libation, grant to our minds all fitting things,
 21. and life of prosperous spirit, and together queenly Hygieia,
 22. and Eirene, the child-nurturing god, gloriously honored,
 23. and a life ever flourishing with cheerful counsels.

HYMN 21: Νεφελῶν
----------------

HYMN 23: Νηρέως

HYMN 25: Πρωτέως
----------------

  1. I call upon Proteus, holding the keys of the sea,

  8. fire and water and earth and Aither, and Night and Day,
  9. and Metis, the first begetter, and Eros much-delighting;
 10. for in the great body of Zeus all these things lie.
 11. His head, indeed, to behold, and his beautiful faces—
 12. the radiant sky, around which golden tresses
 13. of the gleaming stars, most beautiful, stir and quiver,
 14. and on both sides two bull-like golden horns,
 15. the East and the West, the roads of the heaven-dwelling gods,
 16. and the eyes are Helios and the counter-facing Selene;
 17. and the unerring royal mind is imperishable Aither,
 18. with which indeed he hears all things and takes thought;
 19. nor is there any voice nor shout nor crash nor rumor
 20. that escapes the ears of Zeus, the over-mighty Kronion.
 21. Thus he has an immortal head and understanding;
 22. but his body is all-bright, boundless, city-smashing,
 23. fearless, strong-limbed, over-mighty—thus it is fashioned;
 24. the shoulders and chest and the broad back of the god
 25. are the Air of wide might, and wings sprang forth for him,
 26. with which he flies everywhere; and sacred became his womb,
 27. Gaia the all-mother and the lofty peaks of mountains;
 28. and the middle belt is the deep-sounding swell of sea and main,
 29. and the lowest base, the roots within the earth,
 30. and dank Tartaros and the furthest bounds of Gaia.
 31. And having hidden all things, again into the much-gladdening light
 32. he was about to bring them forth anew from his heart, working wonders.
